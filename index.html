The Tenure-Track Relay

Five teams carry one chemistry PhD student — Maya — through grad school, a postdoc, and the R1 job market. Every round, all five teams work at once, in their own breakout rooms, each making a call without seeing what the others are doing — then the whole class reveals and resolves the round together.

The five teams

The Candidate

plays Maya
  • Makes Maya's real move each round
  • Wants a tenure-track offer without her Wellbeing hitting zero

The PhD Advisor

busy every round
  • Sets a standing reaction policy in Round 1, writes Maya's letter in Round 2, makes a final move in Round 3
  • Wants high lab output — her success is a means to that end

The Postdoc Mentor

busy every round
  • Sets a mentoring style in Round 1, reacts to Maya's move in Round 2, makes a final move in Round 3
  • Wants her work to benefit their own lab — her independence is a cost to them

The Funding Panel

busy every round
  • Sets that round's approval policy before knowing if it'll even be used
  • Wants to fund real merit — Maya can be turned down

The Hiring Committee

busy every round
  • Drafts sealed hiring criteria in Round 1, builds a rival candidate in Round 2, makes the final call in Round 3
  • Wants the best fit for the job as they've actually defined it

The Hiring Committee: decide

Now discuss, as a class

  1. Where did merit stop mattering as much as timing or a mentor's mood?
  2. Which team's goal most directly conflicted with Maya's? Did you notice it happening as you played?
  3. Did the Hiring Committee's real criteria match what Round 1 and 2 actually rewarded?
  4. If the Funding Panel had denied every request, how differently would the game have gone?
  5. Whose incentives would you redesign — and how — to make this system work better for the trainee, the science, or both?

Maya reaches burnout
